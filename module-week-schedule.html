CS6515 Weekly Study Plan

Weekly Study Plan

A topic-based study sequence for CS 6515: Intro to Graduate Algorithms. Each week connects lectures and modules with suggested practice and learning goals. Follow the sequence at your own pace.

For quick jumps: view by Week Folder, study by Conceptual Topic, or review continuously on the All Weeks Stacked Page.

Week / Unit Lectures & Modules Suggested Practice Learning Goal
Week 1
Unit 1: DP		 Introduction & Readiness Check
DP1: Fibonacci (FIB), Longest Increasing Subsequence (LIS), Longest Common Subsequence (LCS)
DP2: Knapsack (with/without rep), Matrix Chain Multiplication
👉 Go to Week 1 		Check prerequisite knowledge, then practice defining DP states, recurrences, base cases, fill order, and runtime for LIS, LCS, knapsack, and matrix-chain multiplication. Build clear bottom-up DP solutions.
Week 2
Unit 1: D&C		 DC3: Solving Recurrences (Master Theorem, recursion trees)
DC1: Fast Integer Multiplication (Karatsuba)
👉 Go to Week 2 		Solve recurrences with the Master Theorem and recursion trees; explain how Karatsuba combines three recursive products. Justify recurrence bounds and recursive decomposition.
Week 3
Unit 1: D&C		 DC2: Linear-Time Median & Selection (QuickSelect, Median of Medians)
👉 Go to Week 3 		Trace QuickSelect and median-of-medians, implement selection, and justify the linear-time recurrence. Understand expected and worst-case selection bounds.
Week 4
Unit 1 / 2		 DP3: Shortest Paths on DAGs, Bellman-Ford, Floyd-Warshall
GR1: Strongly Connected Components (SCCs / Kosaraju)
GR2: 2-SAT Formulation & Reachability
👉 Go to Week 4 		Trace DFS and SCC decomposition, build a 2-SAT implication graph, and compare shortest-path dynamic programs. Connect graph structure with reachability and shortest paths.
Week 5
Unit 2: Graphs		 GR3: Minimum Spanning Tree (MST: Kruskal's & Prim's algorithms)
👉 Go to Week 5 		Trace Kruskal's and Prim's algorithms and prove safe edge choices with the cut and cycle properties. Prove greedy edge choices are safe.
Week 6
Unit 2: Max-Flow		 MF1: Ford-Fulkerson, Flow Networks, Residual Graphs
MF2: Max-Flow = Min-Cut Theorem
👉 Go to Week 6 		Construct residual networks, find augmenting paths, check flow conservation, and verify a maximum flow with a minimum cut. Connect flow feasibility, residual paths, and cut certificates.
Week 7
Unit 2: Max-Flow		 MF4: Edmonds-Karp Algorithm & Flow Reductions (Bipartite Matching)
👉 Go to Week 7 		Trace Edmonds-Karp BFS augmentations and reduce bipartite matching to a flow network. Use flow algorithms as components of larger reductions.
Week 8
Unit 3: NP		 NP1: P, NP, Verification, Polynomial-Time Reductions
NP2: 3-SAT, Circuit-SAT
NP3: Graph Problems (Independent Set, Vertex Cover, Clique)
👉 Go to Week 8 		Write polynomial-time reductions with the correct direction and prove both implications for SAT and graph problems. Establish NP-completeness with complete reductions.
Week 9
Unit 3: LP		 LP1: Linear Programming Formulation & Modeling
LP2: LP Geometry, Vertices, Simplex Algorithm
LP3: LP Duality (Weak/Strong Duality, Dual Certificates)
👉 Go to Week 9 		Formulate linear programs, derive duals, interpret feasible vertices, and verify bounds with dual certificates. Connect primal feasibility with dual bounds.
Week 10
Unit 3: Approx		 LP4: Max-SAT 7/8 Approximation & LP Rounding / ILP
NP4: Knapsack Approximation (FPTAS)
NP5: Halting Problem & Undecidability (Diagonalization)
👉 Go to Week 10 		Analyze approximation guarantees, compare pseudo-polynomial algorithms with FPTAS, and explain undecidability proofs. Distinguish approximation, hardness, and undecidability.
Week 11
Unit 4: Review		 FFT: Fast Fourier Transform (DC4/5)
RA1–RA3: Modular Arithmetic, RSA Cryptosystem, Bloom Filters
Flow & Graph Applications
👉 Go to Week 11 		Connect FFT, modular arithmetic, RSA, Bloom filters, and flow applications, then review the weekly topics together. Synthesize advanced applications and prior topics.
