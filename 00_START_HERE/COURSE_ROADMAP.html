Unit 1: Dynamic Programming & Divide-and-Conquer (Exam 1)

The first unit focuses on algorithmic design principles where problems exhibit optimal substructure or recursive decomposition.

  • Week 1 — Intro & Dynamic Programming (DP1, DP2): Fibonacci, Longest Increasing Subsequence (LIS), Longest Common Subsequence (LCS), Knapsack (with and without repetition), and Matrix Chain Multiplication.
    👉 Go to Week 1 · DP Guidance · Recurrence Relations
  • Week 2 — Divide & Conquer Foundations (DC3, DC1): Solving recurrences with the Master Theorem, recursion trees, and Karatsuba fast integer multiplication.
    👉 Go to Week 2 · D&C Guidance · Solving Recurrences
  • Week 3 — Linear-Time Median & Selection (DC2): QuickSelect, expected (n)$ randomized selection, and worst-case (n)$ Median of Medians.
    👉 Go to Week 3 · Usable D&C Algorithms
  • Week 4 (DP Portion) — DP on DAGs & Shortest Paths (DP3): Bellman-Ford, DAG shortest paths, Floyd-Warshall, and all-pairs shortest paths.
    👉 Go to Week 4

Exam 1 Checkpoint: Covers content from Weeks 1–3 (DP1–DP2, DC1–DC3). DP answers must follow the strict 7-step bottom-up format.

Unit 2: Graph Algorithms & Max-Flow (Exam 2)

The second unit transitions into graph representations, depth-first search properties, and network flow optimization.

  • Week 4 (Graph Portion) — Strongly Connected Components & 2-SAT (GR1, GR2): DFS pre/post numbering, Kosaraju's 2-pass algorithm, DAG of SCCs, and reduction of 2-SAT to SCC reachability.
    👉 Go to Week 4 · Graph Theory Guidance · Usable Graph Black Boxes
  • Week 5 — Minimum Spanning Trees (GR3): Cut property, cycle property, Kruskal's algorithm (with Union-Find), and Prim's algorithm.
    👉 Go to Week 5
  • Week 6 — Max-Flow Basics & Min-Cut (MF1, MF2): Flow networks, capacity constraints, flow conservation, Ford-Fulkerson algorithm, residual graphs, augmenting paths, and the Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem.
    👉 Go to Week 6
  • Week 7 — Edmonds-Karp & Flow Reductions (MF4): Shortest augmenting path rule ((m^2 n)$), bipartite matching, vertex capacities, and reductions to max-flow black boxes.
    👉 Go to Week 7

Exam 2 Checkpoint: Covers content from Weeks 4–7 (DP3, GR1–GR3, MF1–MF2, MF4). Standard graph algorithms and Ford-Fulkerson must be used as black boxes without internal modifications.

Unit 3: NP-Completeness & Linear Programming (Exam 3)

The third unit addresses computational complexity (what problems cannot be solved efficiently) and general continuous optimization (linear programming).

  • Week 8 — NP-Completeness & Reductions (NP1, NP2, NP3): P vs NP, verification, polynomial-time reductions, Circuit-SAT, 3-SAT, Independent Set, Vertex Cover, and Clique.
    👉 Go to Week 8 · Common Course Runtimes
  • Week 9 — Linear Programming Foundations (LP1, LP2, LP3): Formulating linear programs, geometric intuition, vertices/extreme points, standard and slack form, Simplex algorithm, and LP Duality (weak and strong duality).
    👉 Go to Week 9
  • Week 10 — Approximation, ILP & Undecidability (LP4, NP4, NP5): Integer Linear Programming, LP relaxation and rounding, Max-SAT 7/8 approximation, Knapsack FPTAS, and the Halting Problem (undecidability & diagonalization).
    👉 Go to Week 10

Exam 3 Checkpoint: Covers content from Weeks 8–10 (NP1–NP5, LP1–LP4). Ensure reduction directions are correct ( \le_p B$ means transforming instance of known hard problem $ into $).

Unit 4: Advanced Topics & Cumulative Review

  • Week 11 — Advanced Grab-Bag (FFT, Modular Arithmetic, RSA, Bloom Filters): Divide-and-conquer polynomial multiplication via Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), Euler's totient, modular exponentiation, RSA cryptosystem, and probabilistic Bloom filters.
